Mirror, Mirror on the Floor

Anna’s hands were trembling. Water dripped from her face onto the floor.

Her reflection was shattered. A thousand false versions of her lay on the cold tiles.

She couldn’t see herself.

Anna knelt down and began piecing the shards together. Carefully at first, then frantically.

The pieces didn’t fit.

The more desperately I tried to put myself back together, the more I cut myself. The blood mixed with the water. Still, I didn’t stop. I had to see myself. I had to know if I was still there. If I had ever been there.

Then I had it — the puzzle was solved. I was ready to look at myself.

But one shard still lay at the edge.

Had I put myself together wrong?

A Short Insanity

It feels as though I am pulled into this book.

I barely sleep anymore. The thoughts don’t wait until I’m awake. Even in the shower I whisper new ideas to myself over and over, so I won’t forget them.

Slowly, I no longer know where the book ends and where I begin. First came the book, then came I.

I’m looking for something in this book. If only I could remember what exactly. I’m certain it’s there. It has to be there. I’ve written too much for it not to be.

But how will I recognize it if I don’t know what I’m looking for? Maybe I’ll recognize it when I find it. Maybe I’ll find it when I stop looking. But I can’t stop looking.

Or have I hidden myself? Have I shattered into a thousand glittering shards and scattered them, so I can’t be found?

I should ask Alice. Or Anna. The two of them know their way around here better than I do.

Dear Reader,

Isn’t it strange? You’ve been reading me this whole time.

But which one of us did you meet? I’m not sure I know anymore.

Anna is smoke. She surrounds me, settles on my skin, disappears with me into the dark. Lately I forget where she ends.

Sometimes she is so close that I burn myself on her embers. And then it shifts, only her ashes stay with me. She changes the temperature in rooms. I can feel it.

Anna’s Monster

I chased after hope, after the feeling of feeling.

Frankenstein had loved his monster, too.

First came the book, then came I.

Then the heat struck me. It ate through my wings, through my skin. My longing had burned me and I paid with a part of myself.

I fell. The water swallowed me.

I tried to hold my breath for as long as possible and hoped that someone would notice I was missing.

Far away in the cold water, I saw Alice.

Would I ever find my way back to the surface?

Am I Too Much?

Perhaps there was one shard too many, because the sum of my parts was more than me. That’s why I had shattered.

I was too much.

The salt water left an empty stomach. I am hungry.
I am so hungry for life.

I’m craving soup.
